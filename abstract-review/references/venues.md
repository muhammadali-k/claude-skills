# Venue standards for abstracts

Check the target venue's rules and flag violations. For a *working draft* note submission-only steps
(e.g. formatting) rather than stripping them prematurely. Limits change year to year — if the user names a
venue and year, prefer the current call-for-abstracts over the numbers below, which are typical values.

## Hematology / oncology congresses

### ASH (American Society of Hematology) — Confex
- **Body limit:** ~**4,500 characters including spaces**, excluding title, authors, and affiliations.
  Count the accepted-text body (`render OUT.docx accept`), minus the title line.
- **Structure:** Introduction / Methods / Results / Conclusion (or unstructured within the limit).
- **Formatting (on submission):** **no bold or underline in the body** (italics allowed); **no tables or
  figures** in the text abstract; a single figure/table may be a separate upload in some categories —
  check the year's rules. Bold section headers are fine in a working draft but must be plain text on
  submission — note this rather than removing it early.
- **Abbreviations:** define at first use.

### ASCO (American Society of Clinical Oncology)
- **Body limit:** ~**2,000 characters** (tighter than ASH) plus separate limits for title and author
  block; one table permitted in most tracks (counts toward a separate allowance).
- Structured Background / Methods / Results / Conclusions. Trial registration number often required for
  interventional work.

### EHA (European Hematology Association)
- **Body limit:** ~**2,500–3,000 characters** including spaces; structured; one image/table often allowed.
  Confirm the year's figure.

### ESMO / ASCO-GU / other oncology congresses
- Character-limited, structured, often one table. Confirm per-congress.

## Journals (structured abstracts)

- **Word limit** (not character): commonly **250–300 words**; some (JAMA family) up to **350** with a
  fixed structured format (Importance/Objective/Design/…/Conclusions and Relevance). Trial abstracts:
  follow **CONSORT for Abstracts**. Systematic-review abstracts: **PRISMA for Abstracts** (12 items).
- Register/trial number, funding, and a data-availability line may be required in the abstract or
  immediately after.

## Generic checks (any venue)

- Count the **accepted** body, not the marked-up one, against the limit; report the number and headroom.
- Structured-section headings present and in the venue's order.
- Every abbreviation defined at first use in the abstract itself (abstracts are read standalone).
- No reference citations inside most abstracts unless the venue allows them.
- Numbers reported with consistent precision and matching CIs.

## How to surface venue issues

- **Over the limit:** flag with the current count and the target, and suggest the highest-value cuts
  (usually redundancy, not results).
- **Formatting banned on submission:** a single comment listing the submission-time steps (unbold headers,
  remove any table, etc.) — as a checklist, not tracked edits on the working draft.
- **Missing required element** (registration number, structured heading): a comment naming the specific
  requirement.
