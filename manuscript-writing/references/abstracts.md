# Abstract playbook

How Khan writes conference and grant abstracts. Read before drafting an abstract. Abstracts compress the full voice — same funnel, same enumeration instincts, same standing caveat — into a tight, heading-driven form.

## Heading set — match the venue

Khan writes to **explicit labeled headings and hits them in strict IMRaD order every time**. Choose the set for the venue:

- **Conference abstracts:** bare **Background / Methods / Results / Conclusions** (singular *Conclusion* only occasionally).
- **Full / AMIA-style versions:** add combined headings — *"Introduction and Background:", "Discussion and Implementation:"*.
- **European Urology manuscripts:** **Context / Objective / Evidence Acquisition / Evidence Synthesis / Conclusion / Patient summary** (add the lay Patient summary when required).
- **Grant deliverables:** a **"Scientific Abstract"** plus a plain-language **"Lay Abstract"** split into **Purpose / Approach / Importance**.

Word budget: in conference versions, **Methods and Results carry the density**; Conclusions is a single sentence.

## Background — a two-sentence funnel

Often **exactly two sentences**:
- **Sentence 1** states the accepted premise/importance.
- **Sentence 2** pivots on *"However,"* / *"Although"* to the specific gap (*"remain poorly characterized", "remains unclear", "little is known about"*), frequently **closing by naming the study's own response with a first-person verb** (*"we present…", "We updated…", "we aim to…"*).

The **objective is folded into Background**, not given its own line.

Example: *"Although progression to myelofibrosis (MF) confers worse prognosis and informs treatment decisions, predictors of fibrotic progression in polycythemia vera (PV) and essential thrombocythemia (ET) remain poorly characterized."*

## Methods — the longest, densest section

A **single paragraph marching strictly chronologically**:
data source (with exact provenance — PMID, specific search cutoff dates like *"through July 1, 2026"*) → cohort / N and cohort composition → variables enumerated near-exhaustively in parentheses → train/test or development/evaluation split as **counts and percentages** → named statistical machinery → **significance threshold as a closing clause**.

## Results — total N first, table last

**Open with the total N** (*"A total of 505 PV and 198 ET patients were identified"*), give demographics, then report each estimate with the **full inline triple** (point estimate; 95% CI; p), and **end by pointing to the table** — *"Results are summarized in Table." / "(Table)."*.

## Conclusions — one hedged sentence + one forward sentence

Restate the **single headline finding in one hedged sentence** (*"may"*) plus **one forward-looking clinical-utility or validation sentence**. The Conclusion should paraphrase the manuscript Conclusion and match its standing caveat.

## How abstracts differ from the manuscript

- **Drop** the literature-comparison and mechanistic digressions.
- **No reference list** — cite by bare parenthetical PMID and by naming trials/guidelines/instruments with acronyms.
- **Weave all numbers into prose** plus one appended results **Table with a full caption and abbreviation key**. Don't offload the headline estimates entirely to the table — the prose must carry them.
- **Unstructured within each section** — one block per heading, no internal paragraph breaks.
- **Author byline:** Khan first with superscript institutional affiliations; senior authorship shared between **Irbaz Bin Riaz** and **Jeanne M. Palmer** (star-marked), one of them in the last slot (Palmer last on MPN/heme abstracts, Irbaz on GU/AI).

## Deriving an abstract from a finished manuscript

Write the abstract **from the finished manuscript, not before it**. Pull the total-N opener from Results, the headline triples from the effects table, and paraphrase the manuscript Conclusion for the abstract Conclusion (keeping the same standing caveat). Then choose the venue's heading set and append the one results Table.
