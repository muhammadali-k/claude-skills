# Geographic preferences & program signals (ERAS)

Two distinct ERAS mechanisms. Don't conflate them.

## Geographic preferences

In the ERAS supplemental data, an applicant flags a small number of **divisions** they prefer (a map of ~9 U.S. census-style divisions: New England, Middle Atlantic, East/West North Central, South Atlantic, East/West South Central, Mountain, Pacific). It is a **soft signal of interest to programs, not a filter.** The applicant can — and often should — **apply to and signal programs outside** their preference divisions. So when researching, look beyond the chosen divisions for standout programs (especially strong universities), and don't drop a great fit just because it's in another division.

Help the applicant pick divisions by weighing, per division: how many genuinely IMG-friendly programs it has, academic strength, representation of the applicant's affinity group, target-subspecialty fellowship density, and any personal ties (home base, an institution where they already work). A division can be individually high-yield for one applicant (e.g., where they already do research) even if it's thin on paper.

## Program signals

Signals tell a program "you're genuinely interested." **The count is specialty-specific and changes year to year — look it up for the current cycle** (AAMC "Program Signals" overview; specialty societies like AAIM for Internal Medicine). Some specialties use a **flat** number of signals; others use **tiered Gold/Silver** signals, where Gold conveys stronger interest.

- Example (verify for the current cycle): **Internal Medicine 2026-27 = 3 Gold + 12 Silver.**
- Programs are advised to weight signals for **interview invitations, not rank-list order.** So a signal's entire value is getting the applicant *into the interview pile*.

### Allocation strategy

The goal is to spend each signal where it changes the outcome:

- **Spend on Reach and Target programs**, not near-certain safeties. A strong applicant will be interviewed at safeties without a signal, so a signal there is wasted. (Residency Explorer's "Gold Signal vs No Signal" interview-rate columns show exactly how much a signal lifts odds at each program — spend where the lift is large.)
- **Gold** (if the specialty tiers signals) goes to **genuine top choices** where a signal is decisive **and/or** where the applicant has a real connection — a person they know, an institution they already work at, or a resident/faculty member from their **own medical school**. Those are the highest-yield places to spend the strongest signal.
- **Match the overall mix to the applicant's risk tolerance.** If they want a "safe" portfolio, most signals should be realistic targets (verified to take their profile), with only a couple of reaches. If they're swinging for prestige, weight toward reaches — but say plainly that it trades against safety.
- **Honor hard constraints.** If the applicant declares a program a non-negotiable signal, keep it and allocate the rest around it.

### Common tension to surface

"Good university (for a physician-scientist)" and "safe" often pull in opposite directions — the strongest academic programs take the fewest IMGs. Resolve it by finding **university or university-affiliated programs that verifiably take a meaningful share of non-US IMGs** (these are the safe-academic sweet spot), and by being explicit about which signals are the reaches. Present the tradeoff; let the applicant choose.
